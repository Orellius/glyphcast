how it works

  1. source
  2. sample
  3. encode cells
  4. delta-pack
  5. websocket
  6. unpack
  7. one GL draw

Every frame is a grid of monospace cells. A cell is one glyph — octant (2×4 subpixels, U+1CD00), sextant (2×3), quadrant (2×2), halfblock, or an ascii ramp — plus a foreground and background color. Frames go over the wire as skip/emit runs against the previous grid: byte-aligned, no entropy coding, dumb enough for a terminal or an MCU to decode. The wire never carries pixels.

a terminal is a full receiver

$ GC_CH=verify GC_FRAMES=120 bun clients/term.ts
{"frames":120,"checksum":2291410159,"cols":240,"rows":81}

The same stream the browser draws, rendered with ANSI truecolor escapes — real U+1CD00 octant glyphs in your terminal. The checksum matches the caster's: the wire is lossless by design. That output is a real end-to-end run, not a mock.
